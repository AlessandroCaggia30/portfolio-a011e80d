Working Paper · Agent-Based Simulation of Firm Incentives

Emergent Effort, Wage Dispersion, and Supervisor Collusion in a Heterogeneous-Worker Firm

Firm ABM v3 · ODD-compliant · Nash microfoundations · empirical validation
Abstract. We present an agent-based model of a firm with heterogeneous workers, a middle-management (supervisor) tier, and a principal, following the ODD protocol (Grimm et al. 2020). Worker best-response effort is derived from primary-source Nash equations (Holmström-Milgrom 1987; Holmström 1982; Kandel-Lazear 1992; Shapiro-Stiglitz 1984; Lazear-Rosen 1981; MacLeod 2003; Tirole 1986). The two behavioural modifiers over Nash — reciprocity (Fehr-Falk 2002) and peer conformity (BBR 2005 §V) — are calibrated to published empirical coefficients. We report emergent distributions of effort, wage Gini, cooperation intensities, and Tirole-1986 collusion rates across nine contract designs A0–A8. The simulation reproduces four published empirical stylised facts: BBR (2005) piece-rate effort response, Kandel-Lazear (1992) peer-pressure attenuation, MacLeod (2003) subjective-bonus collapse under noise, and Tirole (1986) contract-dependent collusion. All statistics reported with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over 25 seeds.

1 · Introduction

The incentive-design literature has produced a rich set of analytical Nash predictions — Holmström (1979); Holmström & Milgrom (1987); Holmström (1982); Kandel & Lazear (1992); Shapiro & Stiglitz (1984); Lazear & Rosen (1981); MacLeod (2003); Tirole (1986). Every one is proved under a representative-agent assumption. The empirical firm literature (Prendergast 1999, 2002; BBR 2005-2010; Bloom et al. 2013) documents that observed behaviour departs from these predictions in specific ways: high-powered incentives are rare, subjective bonuses dominate, peer effects are large.

This paper builds an agent-based model that (i) enforces primary-source Nash best responses at every step, (ii) allows heterogeneity in the parameters that analytical models fix, (iii) reports emergent distributions (not just means) with 95% bootstrap CIs, and (iv) validates against four published empirical target moments before drawing conclusions. The contribution is the exact one Chang & Harrington (2006, HCE Vol. 2) identify as missing: an ABM at the sophistication of Tirole (1986) with proper heterogeneity and empirical validation.

We answer four research questions organised around the ODD protocol:

  1. Q1 — Scale: how does emergent effort scale with N under each contract, and how well does the Kandel–Lazear (1992) analytical prediction fit?
  2. Q2 — Rating noise: at what noise level does the MacLeod (2003) subjective bonus collapse?
  3. Q3 — Supervisor collusion: which contracts induce the most Tirole (1986) collusion?
  4. Q4 — ATE bias: which contract structure gives the least-biased naïve difference-in-means estimator?

2 · ODD · Overview

Purpose

Simulate a firm as a game-theoretic system where heterogeneous workers, a middle-management (supervisor) layer, and a principal interact under different incentive contracts, and observe emergent effort distributions, wage dispersion, cooperation, supervisor collusion, and treatment-effect bias.

Scheduling

Discrete periods (≈ one calendar month). Per period, in strict order:

  1. Contract announcement (Principal).
  2. Worker best-response effort (each Worker maximises expected certainty-equivalent utility).
  3. Production shocks realise (Nature).
  4. Supervisor observation with skill-scaled noise.
  5. Tirole (1986) side-payment collusion game.
  6. Audits with probability p; reputation updates.
  7. Wages paid; reference wages update.

3 · ODD · Design concepts

Basic principles

Rational best-response drawn from a primary published source; no hand-crafted effort rules. Two additive modifiers over Nash — reciprocity (Fehr-Falk 2002) and peer conformity (BBR 2005 §V) — are calibrated to published empirical coefficients.

Emergence — what is not pre-specified

  • Effort levels — emerge from the interaction of contract × individual heterogeneity × peer effects × reciprocity.
  • Wage dispersion — emerges from ability × contract × noise.
  • Collusion patterns — emerge from the Tirole side-payment IR condition under different (β, p_audit, worker rent) triples.
  • ATE bias — emerges from the interaction of treatment with contract-induced effort reallocation.

Objectives

Worker maximises CARA certainty-equivalent U = α + βge − ½ke² − ½ρβ²σ² (Holmström-Milgrom 1987 §3).
Supervisor maximises wage + side_payment − monitoring_cost − expected(penalty · reputation shock).
Principal maximises Σ output − Σ wages − Σ audit costs.

Stochasticity

  1. Attribute draws at t=0 from normal / beta priors (§ 5).
  2. Production noise σ_output every period.
  3. Supervisor observation noise σ_sup / θ every period.
  4. Audit indicator ~ Bernoulli(p_audit).
  5. Collusion decision under Tirole IR: Bernoulli(1 − integrity).

9 · Emergent wage dispersion

Wage Gini is a first-order emergent property because ability, contract, and observation noise interact in nonlinear ways. Under flat wage (A0) Gini ≈ 0. Under tournaments (A2) Gini is bimodal (winners vs losers). Piece rates (A1) yield a smoothly-decreasing distribution.

12 · Q3 — Tirole (1986) collusion

Under contracts with β > 0 (piece rate, tournament, team pay), the worker has a positive rent from under-reporting productivity. Following Tirole (1986 Prop. 1), the supervisor accepts a side payment iff s ≥ ½(worker rent + supervisor reservation + p_audit · penalty); audits break collusion iff p_audit · penalty ≥ worker rent. We measure the emergent collusion rate.

13 · Q4 — ATE bias per contract

We assign a productivity-augmenting treatment (structural τ = 20%) to a random 50% of workers. The naïve difference-in-means estimator should recover τ. Under high-powered contracts it does not, because treated workers reallocate effort into the paid dimension (Holmström-Milgrom 1991 multitasking).

14 · Discussion

What the simulation reproduces. Under the current calibration, all four validation moments are satisfied within tolerance: BBR (2005) piece-rate effort response, Kandel-Lazear (1992) peer-pressure attenuation, MacLeod (2003) subjective-bonus noise collapse, Tirole (1986) contract-dependent collusion. The emergent effort distributions have the qualitative shapes predicted by the underlying theories.

Where the ABM adds value over analytical models. Analytical Nash predictions give point estimates for a representative worker; the ABM gives full distributions across a heterogeneous population. The variance and shape of these distributions matter for policy — e.g. under A1 the mean effort satisfies HM 1987, but the distribution reveals ~15% of workers exert near-zero effort even under a piece rate, because they draw high k.

Limitations to be addressed in v4.

  1. Fictitious-play convergence within period is not iterated to fixed-point; Duffy (2006, HCE Vol. 2) shows this takes 5-50 iterations.
  2. Peer network is static; Gemkow-Neugart (2011) endogenous-network extension would be a natural addition.
  3. Supervisor's monitoring intensity is fixed; a fuller Tirole (1986) game would endogenise q.
  4. Principal does not optimise contract choice; treating each A_j as exogenous is a simplification.

Personnel economics · ABM

How much productivity does personnel AI unlock in a firm?

A computational proof-of-concept for Quindi. The ABM is the digital twin where we know the ground truth. We ask: what is the value ceiling of putting the right worker on the right task under the right contract — and how much of that ceiling survives noisy AI in practice?
01

The question, in one sentence

Central question
Can an AI that observes worker-task fit better than a human manager causally raise firm output, and by how much — decomposed into what workers do (intensive margin) and who is doing it (assignment margin)?

This maps onto two of the five classical dimensions of personnel economics (Lazear & Oyer 2007): organisation of work (which task does each worker perform?) and incentives (under what contract?). The other three dimensions — matching, skill development, teamwork — are future work.

Why this question matters

Lazear (2000, AER) showed that switching Safelite Glass installers from hourly to piece-rate raised productivity 44% — half from workers exerting more effort, half from self-selection into the firm. If a compensation-scheme change alone can produce that gain, an AI that simultaneously optimises assignment and contracts should do much more. Our ABM quantifies exactly how much more, and identifies the conditions under which the gain materialises.

The two Quindi levers

Lever 1

Reorganisation

Reassign each worker to their best-fit task. Formally: choose $\mu: i \mapsto j$ to maximise $\sum_i \alpha_{i,\mu(i)}$. Extensive margin — reallocation gains from putting the right person in the right seat.
Lever 2

Incentives

Assign each worker a contract $A_j$ matched to their type. Piece-rate for those who respond, tournament for those who benefit from relative ranking, team pay for cooperators. Intensive margin — same worker, same task, more effort.
Why both levers, not one

The two levers are not independent. Under flat pay, no worker exerts more than baseline intrinsic effort — so putting them on their best task barely matters. Under piece rates but wrong task assignment, workers exert effort but produce mediocre output. Only when both levers work together does the full comparative advantage of each worker materialise. The interaction of the two levers is the paper's main finding.

The economic mechanism

Under flat pay, workers exert only intrinsic effort — reallocation to a better task raises output modestly. Under piece rates, workers exert Nash effort proportional to their marginal productivity $\alpha_{i,\mu(i)}$ — but if $\mu$ is bad, the marginal productivity is low. Only when both levers align do we get workers on high-fit tasks with high-powered incentives multiplying each other. Neither lever answers its own research question in isolation.

06 · Result 3

Who benefits from each lever?

Workers draw one of four archetypes with distinct productivity distributions across tasks. Each archetype benefits from a different lever mix — Quindi's value is contingent on workforce composition.
Archetype Multiplier (Y11 / Y00) τreorg effect τincent effect Which lever dominates
The teaching takeaway

Specialists — workers with one spike task and mediocre performance elsewhere — gain most from reorganisation. They need to be moved to their spike task before piece rates matter. Generalists gain most from incentives — they can respond well to piece rate on any task. Weak workers barely move under either lever. Quindi's value proposition therefore depends on the client's workforce composition.

06b

Sorting — the Lazear (2000) mechanism

Under piece rate, low-productivity workers quit or are fired; high-productivity workers apply and are hired. The workforce composition shifts. This is a real mechanism from primitives — but the magnitude is calibration-sensitive.
Two claims to keep separate

Emergent (robust): under piece rate, high types stay and low types leave; under flat pay, no sorting operates. This follows from observability (firm sees output only under piece rate) and Nash effort × contract shape. Calibration-dependent (not robust): the exact share of the productivity gain attributable to sorting — Lazear's 50/50 split — depends on fire_rate, outside_option_scale, and sigma_hire.

07 · Result 4

How good does the AI need to be?

Quindi does not observe worker types with perfect precision. It observes $\hat\alpha = \alpha + \eta$ with noise $\eta \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_{AI}^{2})$. We ask: as $\sigma_{AI}$ grows, how much of the value ceiling does Quindi still capture?
Reading: the horizontal axis is AI signal noise, from oracle (left) to random (right). The vertical axis is the fraction of the value ceiling Quindi captures. Below σ ≈ 0.35 capture is ≥85% — the knee of the curve. Above that, capture degrades roughly linearly. Even at random precision, Quindi still captures ~35% of the ceiling because having any contract menu beats uniform flat pay.
The policy takeaway

Investing in AI precision has sharply diminishing returns. Once $\sigma_{AI}$ is below the knee, further improvements are worthless because the ABM's own output noise dominates the AI's marginal signal.

07b · Wage-profit decomposition

How is the surplus split between firm and workers?

Under piece rate, the firm shares surplus with workers to induce effort (Alchian-Demsetz residual-claimant). Once the wage contract is properly PC-binding (Holmström-Milgrom 1987 §III eq. 15), the firm still captures the majority of the productivity gain.
The bug we fixed

Earlier the model set base wage $\alpha = 0.5$ statically in the A1 wage rule, ignoring the participation constraint. Under a proper HM 1987 contract, $\alpha$ must satisfy PC: $$\alpha = \bar U - \beta g e^* + c(e^*) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}$$ i.e., $\alpha$ falls as $\beta$ rises so worker CE stays at reservation. We fixed this in _wage(): the base wage is now the closed-form PC-binding expression, per worker (types varying $\rho_i, k_i$).

The corrected picture

Full-stack (1,1) firm profit is now +37% above baseline, not −23%. Wage share under piece rate is ~55%, not 74%. This matches Lazear (2000) direction — workers do capture more surplus under piece rate — but the firm remains the residual claimant and gains from every intervention. The dashboard's headline (τ_full ≈ 45% output gain matching Lazear 2000, τ_incent ≈ 17% matching Bloom 2013) is preserved.

Still exaggerated?

The fix only handles A1 and A4. A3, A5-A8 still use static base wages. If the paper covers all 9 contracts, each needs its own PC-binding wage rule. For the current A0-vs-A1 comparison (the load-bearing case), the fix is complete.

08 · Result 5

Which real-world experiment should Quindi run?

The ABM is the ground truth. We simulate four candidate field-experiment designs inside it, and ask which recovers the true τ with least bias and variance. 50 trials per design.
Design Estimand True Bias SE Verdict
The recommendation

For decomposing the two levers separately (needed to price the two products): 2×2 factorial. Only design that identifies τreorg, τincent, and their interaction without bias. For a cheap estimate of total value: staged rollout — but never use staged rollout to attribute per-lever effects. It massively biases the incentive-lever estimate upward because the interaction gets loaded onto whichever lever is deployed second.

Does the finding survive perturbations?

Three cross-cutting sensitivity sweeps. If the complementarity broke under any of these, the paper would be over.
Effort-cost elasticity. Higher $k$ (steeper marginal cost) uniformly lowers the value ceiling — but the complementarity survives across the range.
Output-noise elasticity. More noise makes incentive contracts more expensive to run (higher risk premium), so the ceiling drops.

Endogenous AI precision — how much should Quindi invest?

σAI is a choice, not a constant. Precision costs money. The firm's problem is $\max_\sigma \Pi(\sigma) - C(\sigma)$ where $C$ is a Blackwell-style information cost.
Reading: the blue curve is Quindi's profit as a function of AI signal noise σ (from oracle at σ=0 to random at σ→∞). The dashed lines are cost functions at three information-cost levels. The optimum is where the two curves' vertical distance is largest — that's the firm's rational investment in precision.
Cost level $c_0$ Optimal σ* Net profit at σ* Interpretation
The information-theoretic takeaway

Firms in high-margin industries (low cost of information relative to profit gain) should invest in near-oracle precision. Firms in commoditised industries (information cost dominates) should stick with modest AI investment. This is the Blackwell-informativeness ordering — more informative signals dominate but are costly to produce.

09c

Analytical benchmark — two-type case

The multi-agent ABM cannot be solved in closed form. But a simplified two-type version (specialists + generalists only) admits three provable propositions that anchor the numerical results.
Prop 1

Value ceiling under two types

For fraction $s$ of specialists with spike magnitude $\Delta_\alpha$: $$\tau_{\text{full}} \geq s \cdot \Delta_\alpha \cdot \beta^{*} g/k$$ Verified numerically. Each specialist contributes $\Delta_\alpha$ extra output when correctly assigned, times their piece-rate effort response.
Prop 2

Positive interaction

$\tau_{\text{inter}} > 0 \iff s > 0$ and $\Delta_\alpha > 0$. Verified numerically. If no specialists exist, or their comparative advantage is zero, the two levers are additive with no complementarity. With any specialist heterogeneity, the interaction is strictly positive.
Prop 3

Capture ratio bound

For AI noise $\sigma_{AI} < \Delta_\alpha$: $$C(\sigma_{AI}) \geq s \cdot (1 - \sigma_{AI}/\Delta_\alpha)$$ Verified numerically. Once $\sigma_{AI}$ exceeds the specialist spike, the AI can't distinguish spike tasks from noise.
Consequence

Testable prediction

The interaction $\tau_{\text{inter}}$ should be an increasing function of the empirical dispersion of worker productivity across tasks. Testable against firm-level task-fit dispersion (LinkedIn, Compustat + LEHD, or targeted client surveys).

Methods — model at a glance

Assumptions

Agents

  • Workers risk-averse with CARA utility, private effort, additive Gaussian output noise. Holmström & Milgrom 1987 §I
  • Quadratic effort cost $c(e) = \tfrac{1}{2}ke^{2}$. Holmström 1979
  • Type $(\rho_i, k_i, \phi_i, \delta_i)$ and productivity vector $\alpha_{i,\cdot}$ across $T$ tasks. Roy 1951
  • Intrinsic effort baseline $e \geq \epsilon_0 \cdot \alpha_{i,\mu(i)}/k_i$ even under flat pay. Fama 1980
  • Learning-by-doing: $\alpha_{ij}$ grows on assigned task with effort. Argote & Epple 1990 Science

Firm

  • Risk-neutral principal. Assignment policy $\mu: i \mapsto j$; contract policy $c_i$ per worker.
  • Policy sources: oracle (full-info), Quindi (noisy signal), status-quo (persistence).

Environment

  • Peer network: Watts–Strogatz small-world (k=4, p=0.15). Watts-Strogatz 1998; BBR 2010
  • Supervisor rating noise $\varepsilon_m \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_m^{2})$. MacLeod 2003
  • Supervisor–worker collusion under piece rates; audits at prob $p$. Tirole 1986

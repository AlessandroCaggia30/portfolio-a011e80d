Quindi ABM — Two Levers, One AI

A computational proof-of-concept for a personnel-AI product. The ABM is the "digital twin" where we know the ground truth; we ask (i) how much value each Quindi lever creates, (ii) how much of that value survives realistic signal noise, and (iii) which client-facing experiment recovers those effects with least bias.

The research question

What is the value ceiling of a personnel AI, and how close can a real-world Quindi deployment get to it?

1

Reorganisation lever  extensive margin

Reassign each worker $i$ to their best-fit task $j$ using the worker–task fit matrix $\alpha_{ij}$. Reallocation gains from putting the right person on the right task.

2

Incentives lever  intensive margin

Assign contract $A_j$ per worker matched to their private type $(\rho_i, k_i)$. Same worker on the same task exerts more effort under a contract tuned to them.

AI is the infrastructure delivering both levers, not a third treatment. The ABM asks: if a perfect AI existed, how much value would each lever create? And how much of that survives when the AI's signal has variance $\sigma_{AI}$?

Model — assumptions

Agents

  • Workers are risk-averse with CARA utility, private effort choice, additive Gaussian output noise. Holmström & Milgrom 1987 §I
  • Effort cost is quadratic: $c(e) = \tfrac{1}{2}ke^{2}$. Holmström 1979
  • Each worker $i$ has a private type $(\rho_i, k_i, \phi_i, \delta_i)$ and a productivity vector $\alpha_{i,\cdot}$ giving their fit on each task $j$. Roy 1951; Costinot–Vogel 2010
  • Reciprocity: workers raise effort when the wage exceeds a reference wage. Fehr & Falk 2002

Firm

  • Risk-neutral principal. Chooses (a) assignment $\mu: i \mapsto j$ via an AssignmentPolicy, and (b) contract $c_i \in \{A0, A1, A3, A4, A7\}$ per worker via a ContractPolicy.
  • Both policies can be run at three levels of knowledge: oracle (full-info), Quindi (noisy signal $\hat\alpha, \hat\rho, \hat k$ with variance $\sigma_{AI}^{2}$), status quo (persistence-based, no re-optimisation).

Environment

  • Peer network: Watts–Strogatz small-world (k = 4, p = 0.15). Watts & Strogatz 1998; BBR 2010
  • Supervisor rating noise $\varepsilon_m \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_m^{2})$. MacLeod 2003
  • Supervisor–worker collusion at cost $\kappa$; audit prob. $p$, penalty pen. Tirole 1986

Punchline. Reorganisation alone is nearly worthless (τreorg ≈ 0.6) because under flat pay, workers exert zero effort so task fit does not translate into output. Incentives alone give τincent ≈ 30. The joint effect (τfull ≈ 88) is almost triple the sum of the parts. The complementarity between levers accounts for ~65% of Quindi's total value ceiling. Analytical models cannot produce this because heterogeneous workers × heterogeneous contracts × Nash effort together is exactly what breaks closed-form tractability.

PHASE 2 Capture ratio — how good does the AI have to be?

Sweep $\sigma_{AI}$ over $\{0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35, 0.50, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, \infty\}$. For each level, Quindi runs the noisy version of both policies and we compare its output to the ceiling: capture = (Y_Quindi − Y_00) / (Y_11 − Y_00).

σAIY (Quindi)Capture ratioY minus Y00
Two policy-relevant findings. (1) The "knee" is around $\sigma_{AI} \approx 0.35$–$0.50$; below that Quindi captures $\geq 85\%$ of the ceiling, above that value degrades roughly linearly. Beyond $\sigma_{AI} \approx 0.20$, extra AI precision is worthless — the ABM's own output noise dominates. (2) Even at $\sigma_{AI} \to \infty$ (pure random) Quindi still captures 37% of the ceiling, because most of the marginal value from the incentive lever comes from having any menu at all (moving off uniform A0), not from picking optimally.

PHASE 4 Robustness — does the finding survive?

Three cross-cutting checks on the Phase 1 headline. The claim we care about surviving: reorg × incent complementarity is real and large.

4a — Alpha-structure robustness

Rerun the 2×2 ceiling under three worker–task fit distributions: i.i.d. lognormal, block (workers and tasks cluster into types — Roy–Sattinger), and Roy (two skill dimensions per worker, task weights dot-product). Same seeds, same everything else.

α structureτ_reorgτ_incentτ_fullτ_interactionInteraction share

Interaction is positive and large across all three structures. Block gives the strongest complementarity because within-block workers concentrate on tasks they are especially good at; Roy delivers the highest total value because the skill-tail is heavier. i.i.d. is the least favourable structure and still shows a substantial interaction. The finding is not a fit-distribution artefact.

4b — Network topology robustness

Rerun with Erdős-Rényi (random graph, same expected degree) and pure lattice ring (no rewiring) alongside baseline Watts-Strogatz small-world.

Networkτ_reorgτ_incentτ_fullτ_interaction

Essentially identical results across all three topologies. Under this calibration the peer-conformity channel is not the load-bearing structure — the two-lever value comes from worker-task fit × contract-type fit, not from social spillovers.

4c — Elasticity of τ_full to each parameter

Sweep each parameter individually over a 4-point grid, hold others at baseline, measure the resulting value ceiling.

Key comparative statics. Signal noise σ (τ_full: 80 → 38 as σ: 0.3 → 1.0) and effort-cost curvature k (τ_full: 135 → 20 as k: 0.5 → 2.0) drive most of the value variation. Risk aversion ρ matters moderately. Peer-pressure ψ is essentially flat — Quindi's value does not depend on the contested Kandel-Lazear 1992 calibration.

New emergent finding. Full-stack Quindi produces less wage inequality in the tail than the incentives-only cell (Pareto α ≈ 3.60 vs 2.95; higher α means thinner tail). Reorganisation lifts the floor more than the ceiling — matching or exceeding wages for lower-fit workers previously stuck on the wrong tasks. This runs directly counter to the "personnel AI concentrates rewards at the top" narrative and is testable against Song, Price, Guvenen, Bloom & von Wachter (2019 QJE) firming-up-inequality moments.

Contribution

This ABM produces a comparative static — the reorg × incent complementarity — that analytical contract theory cannot produce, because heterogeneous workers, heterogeneous contracts, and Nash effort must be jointly modelled. That result then serves as ground truth to rank real-world RCT designs, giving Quindi a direct recipe for evaluating its own product with clients.

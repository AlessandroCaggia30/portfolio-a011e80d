Quindi ABM

Two levers, one AI

A computational proof-of-concept for a personnel AI. What is the value ceiling of putting the right worker on the right task under the right contract, and how good does the AI have to be to capture it?

01 · FramingTwo levers with different microfoundations

Quindi is not one product — it is two interventions in one interface. AI is the infrastructure delivering both, not a third treatment. The paper is one joint contribution because the two levers interact: neither answers its own research question in isolation.

Lever 1

Reorganisation

Reassign each worker $i$ to their best-fit task $j$ using the worker–task fit matrix $\alpha_{ij}$. Reallocation gain — extensive margin.

Lever 2

Incentives

Assign contract $A_j$ per worker matched to their private type $(\rho_i, k_i)$. Same worker, same task, more effort — intensive margin.

02 · Firm evolutionPer-period trajectories across the 2×2

The four cells of the factorial correspond to four counterfactual firm histories over 60 periods (30 seeds, 95% CI bands). Each cell converges to a stationary output level; the incentives lever moves the transient dynamics as much as the level.

Reading order: output first — the fan opens by period 5. Effort (top-left below) drives the fan: the full stack goes to ~1.6 while the status-quo baseline stays under 0.4. Wage bill — flat contracts pin wages at the outside option regardless of effort; oracle contracts pay for productivity. Collusion — supervisor–worker side-payments appear only under piece rates (Tirole 1986 signature).

04 · HeterogeneityWho gains from each lever?

Workers draw an archetype from a mixture: generalists (high-median productivity across tasks), specialists (one high spike, mediocre elsewhere), stars (high mean and high spread), weak (uniformly low). N=80 workers, T=10 tasks.

Archetype Multiplier from full stack τ_reorg (under oracle contract) τ_incent (under oracle assign.) Lever that dominates
Quindi's value is contingent on workforce composition. Specialists gain 22× from being put on their spike task with proper incentives — they explain most of the aggregate ceiling. A specialist-heavy client firm gains ~4× more than a generalist-heavy one. Testable against Autor 2013 (JLE) task-complexity data and Deming 2017 (QJE) skill composition.

05 · Practical AI precisionHow much survives noise?

Quindi does not have oracle knowledge. Its inputs are noisy: $\hat\alpha = \alpha + \eta$ with $\eta \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_{AI}^{2})$. We ask: as $\sigma_{AI}$ grows, how much of the value ceiling does the noisy-Quindi policy still capture?

The knee sits around $\sigma_{AI} \approx 0.35$–$0.50$. Below that, Quindi captures ≥85% of the value ceiling — precision beyond that point is worthless because the model's own output noise dominates. Above it, value degrades roughly linearly.

07 · RobustnessDoes the finding survive?

Three cross-cutting sweeps: fit-matrix structure, network topology, elasticities to each key parameter. The complementarity between levers is robust to all three; the level of the ceiling depends most on effort-cost curvature $k$ and signal noise $\sigma$.

08 · MethodsModel at a glance

CARA workers with private effort choice; Nash effort per worker; Watts–Strogatz peer network; supervisor collusion under piece rates. Nine contract families microfounded from primary sources; two policy interfaces (assignment, contract) that Quindi's AI parameterises.

Assumptions

Agents

  • Workers risk-averse with CARA utility, private effort, additive Gaussian output noise. HM 1987 §I
  • Quadratic effort cost $c(e) = \tfrac{1}{2}ke^{2}$. Holmström 1979
  • Type $(\rho_i, k_i, \phi_i, \delta_i)$ and productivity vector $\alpha_{i,\cdot}$ across $T$ tasks. Roy 1951
  • Intrinsic effort baseline $e \geq \epsilon_0 \cdot \alpha_{i,\mu(i)}/k_i$ even under flat pay. Fama 1980; Deci-Ryan 1985

Firm

  • Risk-neutral principal. Assignment policy $\mu: i \mapsto j$; contract policy $c_i$ per worker.
  • Policy sources: oracle (full-info), Quindi (noisy signal $\sigma_{AI}$), status-quo (persistence-based).

Environment

  • Peer network: Watts–Strogatz small-world (k=4, p=0.15). Watts-Strogatz 1998; BBR 2010
  • Supervisor rating noise $\varepsilon_m \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_m^{2})$. MacLeod 2003
  • Supervisor–worker collusion under piece rates; audits at prob $p$, penalty pen. Tirole 1986

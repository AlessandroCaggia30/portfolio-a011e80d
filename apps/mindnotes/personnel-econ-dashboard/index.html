Moral hazard failures — what goes wrong under naïve contracts

Under a flat wage (A0), workers exert zero discretionary effort. Under piece rate (A1), effort rises but six distortions appear. Simulation shows the magnitude of each.

F1 · Measurement noise → risk transfer

Workers demand a wage premium for bearing output noise. Higher σ → lower optimal β under HM 1987.

F2 · Multitasking distortion

When one task is paid and another isn't, effort shifts entirely to the paid one.

F3 · Ratchet effect (Gibbons 1987)

Workers under-produce to keep future quotas low. Effort drops with target-updating frequency.

F4 · Risk transfer to worker

High-powered incentives load noise onto risk-averse workers. Wage variance vs β tradeoff.

F5 · Cooperation collapse (Hamilton–Nickerson–Owan 2003)

Under piece rates workers stop helping each other; team pay reverses this.

F6 · Goodhart bunching

Workers cluster just at the quota threshold; effort collapses beyond it.

Takeaways

  • Small teams (N ≤ 10) + low noise → A7 subjective or A4 team pay dominates.
  • Large teams (N ≥ 50) + high noise → A1 piece rate or A3 efficiency wage needed.
  • Middle regimes → A2 tournament or hybrid stacks.
